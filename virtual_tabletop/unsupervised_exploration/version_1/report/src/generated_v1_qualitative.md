From the evaluation videos (`report/videos/video_r1_*.mp4`, 9 held-out
trials each) and the larger showcases (`showcase_r1_*.mp4`, 8 trials):

- **Hands look natural in every version 1 run.** No finger bends
  backwards. Open hands are flat or slightly curled, and closing hands
  curl from the knuckle with the fingertip following (the DIP coupling).
  Version 0's zig-zag fingers are gone.
- **RND (no teacher)** behaves as in version 0. It sweeps toward the
  block, wraps the fingers around fixed blocks and pushes free blocks
  around the pedestal top or off it. It lifts almost never.
- **A+B** reaches as fast as RND (first touch at about 0.8 s, against
  about 1.5 s for the planned scripted approach). On fixed blocks it
  closes all four fingers against the thumb in a power grasp. It lifts
  only some free blocks (16%), and usually drops them again.
- **A+B+D** is the most active manipulator. When it gets a free block
  off the pedestal, it does not hold it still like the script does. It
  swings the arm out and carries the block away, about 6 cm on average,
  and turns it in the hand by about 0.5 rad. Many of its knock-offs come
  from the same swing done without a secure grasp, so it knocks off
  about half of the free blocks (48%).
- **C and C+D (residual)** inherit the script's approach: the arm rises
  sideways, hovers over the block, descends and sweeps the fingers. The
  residual is about as large as the script's own action (ratio about
  0.9), so the descent wobbles and the finger sweep often closes early
  or off-centre. That gives a touch or pinch, but the block stays on
  the pedestal. C+D finishes the lift in a quarter of free trials, and
  both rarely knock blocks off (10%).
- **Scripted teacher** is the smoothest by far (jerk 0.05, against
  0.2–0.3 for the learners). Its lifts are short and vertical, and it
  never explores beyond its one grasp.
